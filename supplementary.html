2. Per-source breakdown of the mixed-corpora panel

The 100-excerpt Mixed-corpora panel comprises 13 source corpora. Per-source crowd/reference rating means are summarised below for reproducibility. Wilcoxon p-values are computed within source and Benjamini–Hochberg-adjusted across the 13 sources. None survive correction at α = .05.

Across all 200 paired excerpts the mean rating difference is Δ = −0.013 with a percentile bootstrap 95% confidence interval of [−0.12, +0.10] on the 1–7 scale (20,000 resamples over excerpts, from data/per_stem_metrics.csv), so the mean difference is tightly bounded around zero. The distribution is right-skewed. The median Δ is −0.125 and the reference is rated higher on 109 of the 189 non-tied excerpts, while the excerpts where the crowd is preferred are fewer but larger in magnitude. That is the same asymmetry documented per metrical relation in supplementary_analyses/metrical_preference/SUMMARY.md.

3. Per-panel participant demographics

Across the 200-excerpt GlobalTap benchmark and the 240-excerpt validation set, 1,498 tapping participants contributed 36,970 trials, and a separate panel of 130 approved listener raters contributed 6,487 ratings on the 240 validation excerpts. The aggregate demographic summary in the main paper is broken down by panel below.

Recruitment and exclusions (benchmark panel). 2,279 people began a session across the eleven Prolific exports that make up the benchmark panel. 868 did not pass the tapping performance check that follows the practice trials, 139 declined the audiovisual consent step, and 515 left before finishing, leaving 757 completed and approved sessions. Of those, 746 contributed at least one usable trial, which is the participant count reported in the paper. At the trial level, 23,493 of 28,945 recorded trials were usable, where a usable trial is one that was not marked failed by the recording check, the marker analysis, or the asynchronous post-trial check. One of those trials has fewer than three taps after cleaning, which is too few for a period estimate, so the period and phase analyses in §5.2 report 23,492 trials. The per-export breakdown sits with the original exports, which are not redistributed (see data/SOURCES.md); the taps themselves are released in data/taps/. The listener-rating panel retained 130 of 139 starts, and those 130 raters contributed all 6,487 reported ratings, including the MIREX comparisons; there is no second panel behind the reported numbers. A separate 20-participant same-pipeline MIREX study (17 approved) was run as an internal check and is not part of the reported ratings.

Rating trials presented each excerpt's Crowd and Reference versions inside one block, in randomized order within that block, so condition order is not confounded with excerpt.

The eight cases below cover excerpts of varying difficulty: three illustrate the structural divergences described in §3.6 (a phase offset, a tactus-level alternative, and bimodal pooled tapping), one is a high-consensus contrast case, and four are simple-alignment excerpts where the KDE peak tips track the optimizer beat grid throughout the song and Crowd / Reference clicks are closely aligned.

Each video concatenates the Crowd-consensus click overlay, then the Reference-annotation click overlay, back-to-back (~30 s total). If a video fails to load, the same excerpts are available as separate crowd_gs/gt click-overlay MP3s in audio_demos/.

5. Supplementary robustness analyses

Multiplicity. Corrections are applied within each family of tests, Benjamini–Hochberg across the 13 mixed-corpus rating comparisons (§2 above), and across all ten metric comparisons mir_eval returns for madmom versus Beat This!, of which §5.4 tabulates five. The q-values quoted there come from that ten-test family. The excerpt-level associations reported in the paper's §3.3 are uncorrected in the text, and every association that is significant there remains significant after Benjamini–Hochberg correction within that family. The weakest are Ballroom CV (p = .0017) and GlobalMood ISTC (p = .004). The country-panel comparisons in §5.2 are FDR-corrected across all 45 pairs.

5.1 Tapper-count convergence

Convergence was assessed with two complementary measures: correlation between participant-normalized tapping KDEs and F-measure between their extracted peaks. Across all 200 excerpts, two separate groups of 50 reached mean density correlation .814 and peak F-measure .880. Comparing a subsample against the full consensus, which contains that subsample, reached .90 at 40 tappers for density correlation and 30 for peak F-measure, and .985/.971 by 90. The full consensus pools all available tappers (114–120 per excerpt, median 117), more than any subsample tested, so it is at least as stable as the 90-tapper values suggest. These values describe this dataset rather than a universal minimum.

A separate exact-pipeline check passed one split per excerpt through canonical trial cleaning, KDE/peak extraction, and the final grid optimizer. The groups reached mean final-grid F-measure .893 at N=20 and .918 at N=50, while agreement on period class was .850 and .885, so agreeing on the metrical level needed more tappers than agreeing on timing within a level. The complete aggregate results are in supplementary_analyses/convergence/final_grid/.

5.2 Exploratory country-panel analyses

For each excerpt, we compared each panel's typical tapping period with a reference built from the other nine panels' typical (median) period on that same excerpt. Trials were first cleaned with the same adjacent-IOI MAD filter used throughout the paper. Each trial's median inter-tap interval was then classified as double rate (ratio .35–.65), matched rate (.80–1.25), half rate (1.75–2.25), or other/unclear.

Panel differences were clearest in which metrical level listeners chose. Japan showed the least double-rate and most half-rate tapping of any panel (7.5% / 20.1%); Egypt showed the opposite extreme, with the most double-rate and least half-rate tapping (28.1% / 9.4%). In tests that treat each participant as one data point and control for excerpt-to-excerpt differences, metrical choice varied significantly by panel (p<.001). The equivalent omnibus test for tap timing (phase) found no panel effect (p=.152). Of the 45 pairwise panel comparisons, 2 survived FDR correction (Japan–Mexico and Brazil–Japan, both q=.045), so we describe the phase evidence as weak rather than absent (panel_tests/phase_pairwise_panel_tests.csv). Fig. 3 of the paper plots each panel's proportions with 95% participant-clustered bootstrap intervals (panel_tests/country_tactus_choice_proportions_with_ci.csv). These comparisons are between recruitment panels on this sampled excerpt set, not against an expert or culturally authoritative ground truth. We grouped participants by where they were recruited, which may not reflect the music they are enculturated to, and we did not measure how familiar they were with individual excerpts.

We also asked whether tapping coherence is higher when an excerpt's source country matches the panel that tapped it. Using the five source countries with 20 benchmark excerpts each (US, KR, FR, MX, EG) and equal-N ISTC per excerpt-by-panel cell, a model with excerpt and panel fixed effects and excerpt-clustered errors estimated a match effect of +0.066 ISTC units (95% CI [−0.030, +0.161], two-sided p=.179): no reliable own-country advantage. Sensitivity analyses and a country-wise meta-analysis that leans positive but is not well powered are reported in supplementary_analyses/cross_cultural/country_match/COUNTRY_MATCH_SUMMARY.md.

5.3 Complete metrical-preference analysis

This analysis includes all 180 excerpts with independent expert beat references. Excerpts are grouped by the dominant Crowd period relative to the reference. Rating differences are excerpt-level Crowd minus Reference means. An excerpt counts as matched, double, or half rate when its crowd-to-reference period ratio falls within ±0.15 octaves (about ±11%) of 1×, 0.5×, or 2×, and as non-integer otherwise. The trial-level bands in section 5.2 are wider because they classify single noisy trials against the other panels rather than a pooled median against the reference.

5.4 Beat metrics beyond F-measure

Standard mir_eval metrics were computed with the tapping-derived GlobalTap consensus beat sequence — regenerated from the complete 746-participant recruitment panel — as the reference and each tracker's beats as the estimate. The analysis therefore asks how each tracker agrees with the crowd consensus. It does not apply the metrics to unaggregated raw taps. Results use the 198 excerpts evaluable for both trackers. Two Beat This! outputs with fewer than two beats in the 10–25 s window were excluded symmetrically from paired tests.

Metric abbreviations (Davies, Degara & Plumbley, 2009): CML = correct metric level (beats must match the reference level); AML = any metric level (double, half, and offbeat variants are also accepted). The suffix c scores only the longest continuous correct segment, while t scores all correct beats. Information gain measures how peaked the distribution of beat errors is relative to the reference (K–L divergence from a uniform error histogram). All metrics were computed with mir_eval defaults.

5.5 Method parameter rationale

Pipeline settings were selected during development using diagnostic checks and sensitivity analyses on the validation corpora (Ballroom, the mixed corpora, and MIREX), then held fixed across all reported excerpts and corpora, including the GlobalTap benchmark. Varying each setting one at a time by 18–50% across all 240 validation excerpts changed the outputs little: perturbed grids still matched the reported grids at F-measure .947–1.000, with the tactus unchanged in 92.1–100% of cases. These are practical values for our stimuli rather than general defaults. Each setting and the reason for its value are listed in METHOD_PARAMETERS.md.

Full CSVs, exclusion records, and exact scripts for these analyses are available in supplementary_analyses/.
